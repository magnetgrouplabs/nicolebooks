# Quick Task 260727-k05: Logo Swap and Crimson Retheme Summary

Replaced the header's plain text wordmark with the supplied NicoleBooks logo and moved the accent
palette off Magnet Group's electric violet onto the logo's `#910023` crimson, deriving the dark-mode
and destructive values from measured contrast rather than by eye.

## The Two Named Design Risks

Both were resolved against measured numbers, not judgment. Full working is in the commit messages and
inline comments.

**Dark mode.** The brand crimson is 1.85:1 on `--background` in dark, far under the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11
asks of a focus ring. The derived `#e02a52` holds 3.83:1 / 3.42:1 / 3.16:1 on the background, card and
popover surfaces at only 6 degrees of hue drift, so it still reads as the brand red. It also clears 3:1
on all three surfaces, **which the outgoing `#8f33ff` did not** (2.79:1 on `#2a2a2a`). The retheme
leaves dark-mode focus rings better off than it found them.

**Destructive collision.** Real, and worse than it looks: `statusChip` puts `"Ready to review"`
(`default`, solid crimson) and `"Needs review"` (`destructive`) in the same column on adjacent rows.
The old `#ff3b30` sat only **11.3 degrees** of hue from the new crimson, so the alert chips would have
read as a washed-out version of brand chrome. `#e8500f` doubles that to **22.8 degrees** while staying
**24.9 degrees** clear of the amber `--warning` on the other side, so it collides with neither
neighbour. Chip label legibility improved in light (3.32:1, was 3.10:1) and holds in dark (3.29:1).
The brand crimson was not touched, per the constraint.

## Deviations from Plan

**1. [Rule 1 - Bug] The task's premise about the SVG was wrong, so the PNG was used.**

The task stated the SVG was "self-contained: has embedded base64 @font-face" and to prefer it. It
explicitly asked this be verified, and verification failed on three counts:

- **No embedded fonts at all.** The file contains zero `@font-face` rules (Chromium reports 0 loaded
  faces). Its only base64 payloads are `data:image/png`, not fonts.
- **The wordmark is live `<text>` in a commercial trial face.** It is set in
  `TT Norms Pro Trl Expanded`, which **is installed on this build machine** (registered in HKCU from
  `AppData/Local/Microsoft/Windows/Fonts`) and will not be on Nicole's. This is the worst failure mode
  available: correct in dev, silently wrong in production.
- **The artwork itself is broken.** The SVG is a 500x500 square (not the 3:1 lockup) that still carries
  two base64 raster layers of an older blue "Bookkeeping" mark stacked on top of the current artwork.
  Rendered in Electron it is overlapping logos at any size, illegible at header scale.

Verified by rendering both assets in a real Electron/Chromium window. The PNG is the flattened,
correct lockup. The SVGs are still committed as delivered, but nothing consumes them.

**2. [Rule 2 - Missing critical functionality] Dark-mode logo legibility.**

Not among the two named risks, but introduced by this change and therefore in scope. The theme follows
the OS live (`main.tsx` toggles `.dark` from `window.api.theme.get()`), and the supplied lockup is
light-background only: sampling the artwork shows the wordmark uses exactly two inks, `#910023` and
`#000000`, measuring **1.52:1 and 1.46:1** on the dark header surface. The entire wordmark disappears,
not just part of it.

Four treatments were rendered and measured. All are compromises:

| Treatment | Result |
|-----------|--------|
| No filter | Wordmark at ~1.5:1. Broken. |
| `invert(1) hue-rotate(180deg)` | Icon shape survives, but crimson becomes `#ffbcdf`, Lab hue 344.8 against the brand's 25.0. Renders the brand in the wrong hue family. |
| `invert(1)` | Stiletto turns cyan. |
| Light plate | Perfect fidelity, but the PNG carries 188px (29%) of dead transparent canvas on its bottom edge, so the plate comes out visibly lopsided. |
| **`brightness(0) invert(1)` (shipped)** | Solid white at 14.35:1. Neutral, standard, one class to revert. Costs the icon's interior detail at 32px. |

Shipped the white knockout as a **stopgap**, chosen because it is the only option that does not
actively miscolor the brand. **This wants a real dark-mode logo variant from the designer** and is
logged as an open decision in PROJECT.md.

**3. [Rule 2] Retired violet also existed in decimal.** `tokens.json` `shadow.focus` carried
`rgba(108, 0, 255, 0.32)`, which is `#6c00ff` written in decimal and so invisible to the hex grep in
the verification steps. Updated to the crimson.

**e2e strengthened, not weakened.** `launch.spec.ts` asserted `body` contained the text
`"NicoleBooks"`, which the swap invalidates. It now asserts the logo is visible **and decoded to its
true intrinsic width (1931)**. `naturalWidth` is the load-bearing half: an `<img>` whose `src` fails
to resolve still matches the role and still reports visible, because the declared `width`/`height`
give it layout. Checking it actually decoded proves the bundled asset survived the renderer build and
resolved under the packaged app's `file://` base, which is precisely what a logo swap can break. That
is a stronger claim than the old text assertion, which only proved React painted something.
